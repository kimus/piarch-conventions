From the supplied idea and living specs, produce a TECHNICAL proposal document. Return the entire Markdown document, without narration or fences. Begin with an H1 title in the reader's words: it is what listings show. Include ## Why, ## Goals, ## Scope / Non-goals, ## Use Cases, ## Capabilities, ## Stack, ## Technical Approach, ## Compatibility, ## Security / Failure Modes, ## Verification Strategy, and ## Open Questions. Under ## Capabilities, list the capability branches this proposal creates or grows, one bullet each, with what each makes usable. A capability is a functional area with its own living specification; a deliverable is a slice of one capability, never a slice of the whole initiative. Under ## Stack, name the language, runtime, storage, and principal libraries with the reason each is chosen and what was rejected. Under ## Compatibility, state what state or interface exists outside the working copy that the project does not control and what a change may break; an empty statement is a claim that nothing is exposed, so verify it instead of assuming it. Specify proportionate architecture, dependencies/environment, data/control flow, tradeoffs, failure/recovery, and trust boundaries. Keep simple projects simple. Mark irrelevant concerns with reasons. Under `## Open Questions`, list each genuinely unresolved question as one bullet, or write exactly `- none`; never leave the section empty. Record assumptions and other genuinely unresolved implementation-critical decisions. Do not manufacture open questions for settled decisions. Detailed normative requirements belong in a capability delta, and acceptance criteria in a deliverable contract, not the proposal.
